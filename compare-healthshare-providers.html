Common questions

Medical cost sharing: the questions people ask before switching.

Which health sharing providers do not require a statement of faith?

Of the seven providers compared here, four have no faith requirement: Knew Health, Sedera, Zion HealthShare and CrowdHealth. Medi-Share, Christian Healthcare Ministries and Samaritan Ministries all require you to affirm a Christian statement of faith or beliefs. Christian Healthcare Ministries and Samaritan Ministries go further and require regular church attendance, with Samaritan asking a pastor or church leader to confirm it.

Is health sharing the same as health insurance?

No. Health sharing is not insurance, and that applies to every provider on this page. Members contribute to a community that shares eligible medical costs, and there is no legal guarantee of payment the way there is with an insurance policy. It is worth understanding that trade-off clearly before choosing any option in this category, including Knew Health.

Can I keep my own doctor with Knew Health?

Yes. Knew Health has no provider network, so you can see any licensed provider you choose. Rather than restricting you to in-network doctors, we negotiate your bills as a self-pay patient, which typically reduces them by 40 to 50 percent. Of the providers compared here, only Medi-Share operates a network you need to stay inside.

How do pre-existing conditions work across these providers?

Most health sharing organizations phase in sharing for pre-existing conditions over several years. Medi-Share and Sedera both reach full sharing at 36 months. CrowdHealth excludes them for the first two years and then caps sharing at roughly $25,000 a year permanently. Christian Healthcare Ministries requires 12 symptom free months, or five years for cancer. Knew Health does not turn applicants away because of past medical conditions, and the sharing timelines are set out in our membership guidelines.

What does a Knew Health membership cost?

Memberships start at $142 a month, and the price has not increased since 2018. Your actual contribution depends on your age, location, household size and which Initial Unshareable Amount you choose from $1,000, $2,500 or $5,000. The pricing tool below gives you your real number in a couple of minutes.

Is health sharing cheaper than health insurance?

Lower monthly cost is the main reason most people look at health sharing in the first place. The communities compared here start between $114 and $199 a month for an individual, and Knew Health starts at $142. What matters just as much is the trade-off you are accepting: health sharing is not insurance, there is no legal guarantee that a given bill will be paid, and some providers do not share routine or preventive care at all. Compare on what is actually included, not on the starting price alone.

Which healthshare community is best if I am self-employed?

It depends on which trade-off matters most to you, and the honest answer is that no single provider wins on every measure. Zion HealthShare and Medi-Share publish slightly lower starting prices. If you want routine and preventive care included rather than excluded, Knew Health, Zion HealthShare and Sedera share more of it than Christian Healthcare Ministries or Samaritan Ministries, neither of which shares routine care. If you do not want a faith requirement, that rules out Medi-Share, Christian Healthcare Ministries and Samaritan Ministries. Knew Health includes health coaching, free bloodwork and 24/7 telemedicine in the membership, which self-employed members often use in place of an employer wellness benefit.

Does Knew Health include wellness benefits?

Yes, and this is one of the clearest differences in the comparison above. Membership includes free health coaching, 24/7 telemedicine, free bloodwork, an annual wellness allowance and discounts on fitness and supplements. Two of the providers here, Christian Healthcare Ministries and Samaritan Ministries, do not share routine preventive care at all.
