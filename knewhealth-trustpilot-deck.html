Snapshot, what Knew Health actually is and how members rate it

What it is
A medical cost-sharing community (not insurance) for health-conscious individuals, families, and the self-employed.
Founded 2018 by Joshua Rosenthal, founder of the Institute for Integrative Nutrition (the world's largest nutrition school). Members share each other's medical bills. Uses an "Initial Unshareable Amount" (IUA) in place of a deductible.

Why this deck exists
Reviews are unusually long and emotional for a healthcare or financial product. That is a content goldmine the brand is barely mining. This deck converts 150 raw reviews into positioning, content, and on-site fixes.

Friction and negative signals (honesty and opportunity)

Geographic limits
"Not great if you live in New York." State-by-state availability is a real blocker and is not surfaced clearly on the site.
One detailed 1-star: a communication failure
A complex claim went silent for over a year, the member was "ghosted," and cancellation met with "unanswered calls." Points to a comms-breakdown failure mode on messy claims.
Jargon fights the customer
Happy members call the IUA a "deductible" and the product "coverage." The brand publicly corrects them in replies. "Supplemental Allowance" was renamed to "Self-Care Credit" mid-stream and confused at least one member.
Portal and UX wishes
Evexia lab platform is "difficult to navigate." Members want claims history "by type" with a more robust dashboard. One prospect reported being told they were ineligible with no follow-up response.

Gap analysis, parts 1 and 2 (of 12)

Gap 01
Speed of reimbursement is buried
Members love this
  • From my initial incident to the bills being paid in full was 33 days.
  • I usually get reimbursement checks within a week or two.
  • Now that I have direct deposit, funds are in my account within days.
What the site does
The exact statistic appears only in a collapsed FAQ entry: "How quickly are expenses shared?" It is invisible to anyone not already searching for it.
What to do
  • Show a single "average reimbursement time" stat above the fold, plain language.
  • Add a visual timeline: submit to share-decision to payout-to-bank.
  • Pull the FAQ answer into the hero so buyers see it before they look for it.
Gap 02
"VIP Concierge" undersells the personal relationship
Members love this
  • I always spoke to the same REAL person.
  • They even called to check on me after I submitted a claim for an ER visit.
What the site does
"VIP Concierge Care Team" sounds corporate. One photo (Hillary). No mention of named continuity or that the same person stays with you through a crisis.
What to do
  • Promise a dedicated, named coordinator in plain language.
  • Feature real coordinator names, photos, and quick bios.
  • Add a quote block from a member about who their coordinator was during a claim.

Gap analysis, parts 3 and 4 (of 12)

Gap 03
"Will they pay for catastrophes?" goes unanswered
Members love this
  • very high
  • above and beyond the IUA.
What the site does
Listed once as "Peace of mind for illness, injury and pregnancy." No on-site proof. The strongest conversion lever in the entire base is invisible.
What to do
  • Build a "Real Members, Real Paid Claims" section with anonymized outcomes.
  • Show event type, dollar band, and days-to-pay, with member first-name and state.
  • Place it directly above the pricing and sign-up CTA.
Gap 04
Medical bill negotiation is not productized
Members love this
  • Pointers on negotiating hospital costs.
  • Working with me on payment plans.
  • Helping navigate getting the real prices (self-pay rates).
What the site does
Mentioned once, under "Proven," in passing. No dedicated section, no stat, no explanation of how it works or who does it.
What to do
  • Promote the 46% reduction to headline status next to payout speed.
  • Add a named "Bill Negotiation Team" with a process explainer.
  • Cross-link from any page mentioning medical bills to this section.

Gap analysis, parts 5 and 6 (of 12)

Gap 05
Proven personas are only implied
Members love this
Eight personas are proven across the review set: self-employed, families, early retirees, between-jobs, maternity, wellness-first, faith-share switchers, and travelers. Each cohort uses specific language for what they need covered.
What the site does
Only one hero quote implies the self-employed. The other seven are not surfaced anywhere. Buyers have to self-identify from generic copy.
What to do
  • Build a persona picker on the homepage ("I am self-employed," "I am between jobs").
  • Ship one landing page per persona with a tailored hook and proof.
  • Add a "Built for you" section that calls out all eight audiences by name.
Gap 06
The wellness flag is planted softly
Members love this
Deep alignment with functional, integrative, and natural health. The CEO founded the world's largest nutrition school. Members describe themselves as grass-fed, organic, supplement-using, thermography-using, and therapeutic massage customers.
What the site does
"Even licensed holistic and integrative providers" appears once. The wellness tribe is left to infer itself from generic copy.
What to do
  • Reposition around "healthcare for health-conscious people," not generic cost-share.
  • Add a wellness-specific page covering holistic, functional, integrative care.
  • Lead with the IIN founder story in the brand narrative.

Gap analysis, parts 7 and 8 (of 12)

Gap 07
"Not faith-based" is hidden as a FAQ
Members love this
  • We switched over to Knew Health after having been members of a religious health share.
  • Accept everyone, regardless of past medical conditions or religious affiliations.
What the site does
"Are you faith-based?" sits in the FAQ. It is not part of the public value proposition, even though it is one of the strongest switcher hooks in the category.
What to do
  • Promote "secular and inclusive" to a hero claim, not a buried FAQ answer.
  • Build a comparison hub hitting "[competitor] alternative" queries for switchers.
  • Capture testimonials from religious-share refugees on a dedicated page.
Gap 08
Maternity is underdeveloped
Members love this
  • Better than insurance for childbirth.
What the site does
Pregnancy appears as one word in the hero benefit list. No dedicated page, no breakdown of what is covered, no stories, no cost examples.
What to do
  • Build a dedicated maternity page covering prenatal, birth, postpartum.
  • Add family cost examples with and without IUA.
  • Capture and publish one or two birth-story videos with member consent.

Gap analysis, parts 9 and 10 (of 12)

Gap 09
Travel and portability are not framed
Members love this
  • Medical crisis while traveling in Mexico was handled.
  • An option that worked everywhere.
What the site does
"Our services work the same way in every state." Implies domestic, not the international portability angle. No traveler or digital-nomad positioning.
What to do
  • Add a traveler and digital-nomad angle to the hero benefits list.
  • Build a "Coverage that travels" explainer page with country examples.
  • Cross-link to it from any FAQ about coverage area or networks.
Gap 10
Trust proof is underbuilt on-site
Members love this
4.8 out of 5 on Trustpilot with roughly 150 reviews, plus an 8-year price lock. The public Trustpilot badge, the count, and the price-lock line are all usable trust signals sitting in external platforms.
What the site does
Only "thousands of members" and the price-lock line. No Trustpilot widget, no star-rating display, no review count, no recent review excerpt.
What to do
  • Embed the Trustpilot score, review count, and one rotating review on every key page.
  • Add a "since 2018, same price" callout next to the price-lock claim.
  • Surface a Trustpilot "verified review" badge in the hero CTA area.

Gap analysis, parts 11 and 12 (of 12)

Gap 11
Day-one wellness value is not staged
Members love this
Self-Care Credit (formerly $250 wellness allowance), free labs via Evexia and FullScript, free 24/7 telemedicine, free health coaching, fitness classes. Members use and value these from day one, even with zero claims.
What the site does
Listed as undifferentiated perks with no dollar values attached. Buyers cannot price the wellness bundle against an insurance alternative.
What to do
  • Frame as "value you use immediately, even with no major claim."
  • Attach dollar figures to each perk ($250 Self-Care Credit, $0 telemedicine, etc.).
  • Add an "Included from day one" section with perk-by-perk breakdown.
Gap 12
The site leads with jargon
Members love this
  • Supplemental Allowance
  • Self-Care Credit
What the site does
Heavy use of Need, IUA, Unshareable Amount, Sharing. The brand publicly corrects members in replies when they use "deductible" or "coverage."
What to do
  • Lead with plain language and map jargon second ("your IUA, think of it like a deductible").
  • Stop correcting members in public replies; mirror their words.
  • Lock terminology changes with internal announcements before renaming perks again.
