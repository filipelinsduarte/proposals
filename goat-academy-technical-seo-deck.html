Older articles are not properly connected through site navigation.

GA-01P0 · Fix first

The category page controls do not create working links to later archive pages.

The archive exists, but Goat Academy's own navigation does not provide a reliable path through the older content.

Impact

Older articles are harder for users and crawlers to move through, and Google has a less reliable route when it returns to recrawl the site.

What the audit found

Most sitemap pages were not reached through Goat Academy's internal links.

550This does not mean Google has not indexed those pages. It means the site's own links did not lead the audit crawler to them.

Why it matters
Internal links explain how pages relate and which content matters. A sitemap should not replace working navigation.
What to fix
Add normal Previous and Next links to every archive page, remove the unresolved placeholder text, and keep article links in the page HTML.

The sitemap sends search engines a mixed and noisy page list.

GA-02P0 · High impact

The sitemap mixes healthy pages with duplicates, broken URLs, redirects, and a noindex page.

It is not acting as a clean source of truth for the pages Goat Academy wants search engines to keep.

Impact

Search engines spend attention checking URLs that should not be submitted, while useful pages become harder to monitor through the sitemap.

What the audit found

The sitemap contains duplicate and invalid entries, while useful pages are missing.

Why it matters
The sitemap should be a simple list of the live pages Goat Academy wants indexed. Mixed signals make that purpose less clear.
What to fix
Use one sitemap system. Keep only live, indexable, canonical pages, remove duplicates and invalid URLs, then add missing pages only when they have a clear search purpose.

Broken and unfinished links create dead ends for visitors and crawlers.

GA-03P0 · High impact

Live pages still link to deleted pages, placeholders, admin screens, and malformed destinations.

Some sit inside valuable guides and programme pages where the next click should build trust or move a visitor forward.

Impact

Visitors can hit dead ends or unfinished calls to action, reducing trust and stopping a course, resource, or consultation journey before it converts.

What the audit found

A small group of live pages creates dozens of broken paths.

Too many pages are indexable without a clear search purpose.

GA-04P1 · Review carefully

Useful guides sit beside thin utilities, quizzes, checkout routes, legacy downloads, and copy-like URLs.

These pages do not all deserve the same treatment in search, but the current setup largely treats them as if they do.

Impact

Internal links and optimisation effort are spread across pages that may never satisfy a useful search need, weakening focus on the content that matters.

What the audit found

Nearly one third of indexable pages need a clear keep, improve, noindex, or consolidate decision.

Why it matters
Every indexable page needs a clear audience, search intent, or business role. Without that decision, the site sends an unclear quality hierarchy.
What to fix
Review pages in batches using Search Console and business data. Keep valuable guides, noindex utilities, consolidate true same-intent copies, and never make bulk decisions from word count alone.

Many pages show the wrong title and description in search.

GA-05P1 · Medium to high impact

Landing-page templates reuse the same title and description across unrelated pages.

A workbook, broker comparison, budget tracker, and research report can all appear under a title describing a completely different offer.

Impact

Search results can look identical or misleading. Users may skip them because they cannot tell what each page offers, reducing click-through rate.

What the audit found

Different pages can appear in search under exactly the same incorrect title.

Why it matters
The title and description are the page's first promise in search. They must describe that exact page, not its template.
What to fix
Remove hard-coded metadata, give each indexable page a specific title and description, use a safe page-name fallback, and fix pages receiving search impressions first.
